Ökonom
In the context of an 1896 German baptismal record from a small village, Ökonom (or Oekonom) translates to farmer, agriculturalist, or landowning farmer.
While the modern word means "economist," 19th-century church and civil records used it as a standard, respectful occupational title for someone who ran a farm.

Key Nuances in 19th-Century Records

• Property Ownership: Unlike a Tagelöhner (day laborer) or Knecht (farmhand), an Ökonom typically owned or held a long-term lease on his own farmland. He managed his own agricultural business rather than working for wages on someone else's land.
• Elevated Social Status: In many rural regions, especially in Bavaria, Franconia, and central Germany, the title carried slightly higher social prestige than the standard word Bauer (peasant/farmer). Pastors and clerks frequently used it for well-established farmers or those managing larger estates.
• Spelling Variations: In late 19th-century handwriting (such as Kurrent script), you will often see it spelled with an "Oe" instead of an umlaut: Oekonom or Oekonomen (which is the dative or genitive grammatical form, meaning "of the farmer" or "to the farmer").

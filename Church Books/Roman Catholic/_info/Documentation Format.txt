Archive-Image Interpretation File

Würzburg archive data history
Physical church books were photographed and microfiche were created. Each microfiche stores about 36 images (Some varieties of microfiche go much higher). At some point the images on the microfiche were scanned to create digital images. Those are the images viewable in the Würzburg archive.

The original church books could be cited by the parish they came from, the book title, the volume number and the page number.
Books were often not page numbered when the data were created. Many page numberings happened later.

The digital images are cited by the fiche they were scanned from. The fiche were cited by the book series  they were generated from. Overall, the Würzburg archive catalogs images by "signatur" number, fiche number and image on the fiche number.

When the physical book was photographed, there were a number of options for content. A book laid out flat has 2 pages showing the left and right. It is not always obvious which side is in an image when only one side is photographed at a time.

The term archive image is used to be more general than saying the fiche image.
There may be archives where the was no fiche intermediate, no microfilm intermediate, but straight to digital.


Process
While at the archive, a researcher will find information about a family member's life event in a church book. The persons mentioned in the event entry are the "Principal Person/s". That information will be viewed at the archive as the "Archive-Image".

At some point after viewing the Archive-Image, and recording the information and location in the church book, an image is taken of the Archive-Image (there may need to be multiple images taken of the same Archive-Image depending on archive's policies).
These images need to be given unique identifiers.

A file is created for each archive-image called the "Archive-Image Interpretation file".

All information about a particular Archive-Image shall be recorded in this file. If multiple images have been taken for a particular Archive-Image, then they shall all be listed and analyzed in the same Archive-Image Interpretation file.

In order to maintain a consistent format, a template file is shown below.

*	The first part of the file contains information about the Archive-Image in its entirety. 
*	Additional sections are added for each image taken of the Archive-Image.
*	Sections at the end are for creating a citation, and for listing links to my TMG database.

Note:
Text in pointy brackets < > is for descriptive purpose and does not appear in the actual data file.
Some words used are predefined keywords from a controlled vocabulary. See the "Church Books Overview.txt" file for definitions.

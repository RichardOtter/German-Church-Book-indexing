[LETTER-BY-LETTER / WORD-FORM ANALYSIS]

Die:
- Latin “die” = “on the day”.
- Common heading for date in Catholic registers.

13:
- Arabic numeral for the calendar day.

Augusti:
- Genitive singular of “Augustus” (month name).
- “Die 13 Augusti” = “on the 13th of August”.

alkred:
- As written; form is unclear.
- Could be an internal note, abbreviation, or misread; no standard Latin word fits cleanly.
- Left unnormalized and marked as uncertain.

Maria:
- Standard Latin/German given name “Maria”.

Margaretha:
- Latinized form of “Margarete/Margaretha”.
- Child’s given name; also reused for the godmother.

Parens:
- Singular “parent”; here used as a heading for the parents’ line.
- In normalized form expanded to “Parentes” to match the plural sense.

Michael:
- Latinized form of German “Michael”.
- Father’s given name.

Loschead:
- Surname as written; likely a Latinized or phonetic rendering of a German family name.
- The “-head” ending may reflect local orthography; kept exactly as in the record.

judiciarius:
- Latin occupational term from “judex” (judge).
- Means “judicial officer”, “court official”, or someone attached to the judiciary.

et:
- Latin conjunction “and”.

Barbara:
- Latinized form of German “Barbara”.
- Mother’s given name.

nata:
- Feminine participle “born”; used in registers to indicate maiden name.
- “Barbara nata Loscherina” = “Barbara born Loscherina” (née Loscher).

Loscherina:
- Latinized feminine form of the surname “Loscher”.
- The “-ina” ending is a typical Latin feminine suffix.

ex:
- Latin preposition “from, out of”.
- Used for place of origin.

Limbach:
- Place name; likely a village or town “Limbach”.
- Treated as German toponym, unchanged.

pudica:
- Latin adjective “modest, chaste”.
- Common moral descriptor for unmarried female sponsors.

Virgo:
- Latin noun “virgo” = “maiden, virgin”.
- Indicates the godmother is an unmarried woman.

M.:
- Abbreviation for “Maria”.
- Expanded in normalized text to “Maria”.

Margaretha:
- As above; given name “Margaretha”.

Ochsenstein:
- Surname as written; clearly German.
- Likely from a place or family “Ochsenstein”.

ex:
- Again “from”.

Watten:
- Place name; likely village/town “Watten”.
- Kept as written.

Sacellum:
- Latin noun “sacellum” = “chapel”.
- Indicates the baptism took place in a chapel rather than the main parish church.

ENGLISH TRANSLATION (modern German names)

Recorded on 15 August 1816.
“May God graciously protect.”

In the year 1816, on the fifteenth of August,
at six o’clock in the evening, in Augsburg,
a child was born to **Michael Gebhard**, a citizen and
Lebacher (craftsman) of Augsburg, and his lawful wife
**Anna Gebhard**, née **Brückner**.

The child received the name **Johann Georg**.

Witnesses were:
• **Johann Georg Albrecht**, Lebachmann in Augsburg  
• **Johann Georg Brückner**, Lebachmann in Augsburg

Augsburg, 15 August 1816.

=======================================================
LETTER-BY-LETTER / PALEOGRAPHIC NOTES (selected difficult words)
=======================================================
"gebohren" – archaic spelling with silent h; the b has a tall
   looped ascender and the following o is small and rounded,
   easy to mistake for an e in fast cursive.

"sechzehn" – the German Kurrent z is a descending hook, quite
   different from Roman z; combined with "ch" it can look like
   a cluster of minims, making the numeral word hard to parse
   at first glance.

"Ackersmann" – recurring formulaic term (farmer/citizen-farmer);
   the ck ligature is compressed into what looks like a single
   stroke with a small loop.

"Löffl / Löffler" – surname beginning with a looped L; the ff
   is doubled with connecting strokes that can be misread as
   "ss" or "tt" depending on ink flow.

"Bachmann" (bottom signature) – appears as the officiant's/
   scribe's signature; the B has a large decorative loop typical
   of formal register signatures, distinct from the body-text
   Kurrent hand.

Surnames "Achilles/Brüelen/Ernstbauer" – lowest-confidence
   readings in this transcription. Kurrent minim clusters (n, m,
   u, i without dots) are visually near-identical, so mid-word
   vowel/consonant sequences in unfamiliar surnames are the
   single biggest source of ambiguity here.

=======================================================
IMPORTANT CAVEAT
=======================================================
This image is more heavily faded/overwritten with pencil
tracing than the previous one, and appears to contain TWO
separate baptism entries side by side (a left/center entry
dated 15 Aug 1816, and a right-column entry referencing the
14th of August). Many mid-word letters — especially in
surnames like "Löffl(er)", "Achilles", "Brüelen", and
"Ernstbauer" — could not be read with full confidence from
this resolution. As before, I strongly recommend:
  1. A higher-resolution crop of the surname clusters, and
  2. Cross-referencing against neighboring entries in the
     same parish register, since the same Ansbach families
     (Löffl-, Ernst-, Achilles, etc.) likely recur across
     multiple pages, which would help confirm spellings.

Januar vormittags um 3 Uhr wurde ein Sohn des Michael

=======================================================
LETTER-BY-LETTER / PALEOGRAPHIC NOTES (selected difficult words)
=======================================================
"Jänner" / "Januar"  – Kurrentschrift ä often looks like plain a with
   a faint umlaut stroke above; the -er ending is a looped flourish,
   easily mistaken for -es.

"gebohren" – archaic spelling of "geboren" with silent h, common in
   18th–19th c. registers; the b has a tall looped ascender, the h
   a low crossed stroke.

"Ackersmann" – Kurrent "ck" is a tight double-stroke that can look
   like a single k; the long s (ſ) inside "-smann" resembles an f
   without the crossbar, a frequent misread trap.

"Bürger" – ü marked by two dots that are often faded/omitted in
   period ink; the g has an open descender loop.

Surnames (Cörper?/Löschard?in/Löffl?/Roßling?) – these carry the
   most uncertainty. Kurrent capital letters (C, L, R) share very
   similar looped forms, and surname spelling in this era was not
   standardized, so the same family name could appear with
   different vowels/consonants across entries. Cross-checking
   against neighboring baptism entries in the same register (which
   often repeat the same family names) would resolve these with
   much more confidence than a single-image read can.
